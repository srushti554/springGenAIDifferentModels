# 1. Purpose

This handbook explains the company's IT policies, services, support procedures, and security requirements.

Employees should refer to this handbook when they need help with company devices, software, accounts, access, security incidents, or other IT-related issues.

# 3. IT Support Ticket Process

Employees should create an IT support ticket for technical issues.

### Process

1. Open the IT Service Portal.
2. Select the appropriate category.
3. Describe the issue clearly.
4. Attach screenshots or error messages when available.
5. Submit the ticket.
6. Track the ticket using the assigned ticket number.

**IT Service Portal:** [https://it.acmetech.example](https://it.acmetech.example)

Employees should avoid submitting multiple tickets for the same issue.

---

# 4. Priority Levels

IT support requests are classified according to their business impact.

## Priority 1 – Critical

Used when a critical business service is unavailable or a major security incident affects multiple employees.

**Examples:**

* Company-wide network outage
* Critical production system outage
* Major security incident

IT will respond to Priority 1 incidents as quickly as possible.

---

## Priority 2 – High

Used when an important business function is significantly affected.

**Examples:**

* Employee cannot access an important business application
* Department-level network issue
* Significant system performance problem

---

## Priority 3 – Medium

Used for issues affecting an individual employee but where a workaround may exist.

**Examples:**

* Application errors
* Printer problems
* VPN connection problems

---

## Priority 4 – Low

Used for general requests and non-urgent assistance.

**Examples:**

* Software installation requests
* General IT questions
* Minor configuration changes

---

# 5. Company Computers and Devices

Company-provided laptops, desktops, monitors, phones, and other IT equipment are company property.

Employees are responsible for:

* Keeping devices secure
* Protecting devices from physical damage
* Using company equipment primarily for authorized business purposes
* Reporting lost or damaged equipment immediately
* Installing only approved software
* Following IT security requirements

Employees must not intentionally modify company hardware or bypass IT security controls.

---

# 6. Software Installation

Employees should install software only through approved company processes.

Software may require IT approval before installation.

Employees must not install:

* Pirated software
* Unauthorized applications
* Unlicensed commercial software
* Software from suspicious or unknown sources

If an employee needs software for work, they should submit a request through the IT Service Portal.

---

# 7. Password Policy

Employees are responsible for protecting their company credentials.

### Requirements

* Use strong and unique passwords.
* Never share passwords with other employees.
* Do not store passwords in unsecured documents.
* Do not use company passwords on personal websites.
* Enable multi-factor authentication when required.
* Report suspected credential compromise immediately.

IT support personnel will never ask employees to disclose their passwords.

---

# 8. Multi-Factor Authentication

Multi-factor authentication (MFA) may be required for company applications and systems.

Employees should:

* Register an approved authentication method.
* Keep their authentication device secure.
* Never approve an unexpected authentication request.
* Report suspicious MFA notifications to IT.

If an employee loses access to their authentication device, they should contact IT Support.

---

# 9. VPN and Remote Access

Employees working remotely may need to connect through the company VPN.

### VPN Guidelines

* Use the company-approved VPN application.
* Do not share VPN credentials.
* Avoid using unsecured public networks when accessing sensitive company systems.
* Disconnect from the VPN when it is no longer required.
* Report repeated VPN failures to IT.

---

# 10. Email Security

Employees should be cautious when receiving unexpected emails.

Do not:

* Open suspicious attachments.
* Click unknown links.
* Provide passwords through email.
* Send confidential information to unauthorized recipients.
* Respond to suspicious requests for money or credentials.

Suspicious emails should be reported to:

**Security Team:** [security@acmetech.example](mailto:security@acmetech.example)

---

# 11. Phishing and Security Incidents

Employees should immediately report suspected security incidents.

Examples include:

* Suspicious emails
* Stolen credentials
* Lost company laptop
* Malware infection
* Unauthorized account access
* Unexpected password-reset notifications
* Suspicious MFA requests
* Accidental disclosure of company information

### Security Incident Contact

**Security Team:** [security@acmetech.example](mailto:security@acmetech.example)
**Security Hotline:** +91 20 4000 5679

Employees should not attempt to investigate or delete evidence from a suspected security incident unless instructed by IT or Security.

---

# 12. Data Protection

Employees must protect company and customer information.

Sensitive information should only be stored in approved company systems.

Employees should not:

* Copy confidential information to personal storage.
* Send sensitive files to personal email accounts.
* Upload confidential company data to unauthorized applications.
* Share confidential information with unauthorized individuals.

If an employee is unsure whether information can be shared, they should contact IT or the Information Security team.

---

# 13. Access Requests

Employees may require access to applications, databases, shared drives, or other systems.

Access requests should be submitted through the IT Service Portal.

Requests may require:

* Manager approval
* Application owner approval
* Security approval

Employees should request only the level of access required to perform their job responsibilities.

---

# 14. New Employee IT Setup

New employees receive IT equipment and system access as part of the onboarding process.

Typical setup may include:

* Company laptop
* Corporate email account
* Employee account
* VPN access
* MFA registration
* Required business applications
* Network access

Managers should submit required access requests before the employee's joining date whenever possible.

---

# 15. Employee Offboarding

When an employee leaves the company, IT will coordinate the removal of system access and collection of company equipment.

Employees are expected to:

* Return company laptops and other IT equipment.
* Return security devices and access cards where applicable.
* Transfer required business files to the appropriate team.
* Stop using company systems after their access is disabled.
* Return company-owned storage devices.

---

# 16. Laptop Loss or Theft

A lost or stolen company device must be reported immediately.

Employees should contact:

**IT Helpdesk:** [it-support@acmetech.example](mailto:it-support@acmetech.example)
**Security Team:** [security@acmetech.example](mailto:security@acmetech.example)

Employees should provide:

* Device type
* Asset ID
* Approximate time of loss
* Location where the device was last seen
* Any other relevant information

IT may remotely lock or wipe the device when supported.

# 17. IT Maintenance

IT may periodically perform maintenance on company systems.

Maintenance may include:

* Operating system updates
* Security patches
* Application upgrades
* Network maintenance
* Server maintenance
* Security configuration changes

Employees may receive advance notifications when maintenance is expected to affect their work.

---

# 18. Backup and Data Recovery

Business-critical information should be stored in company-approved systems that are covered by organizational backup procedures.

Employees should not rely on local laptop storage as the only copy of important business information.

If important data is accidentally deleted or lost, contact IT as soon as possible.

---

Employees should report:

* Lost equipment
